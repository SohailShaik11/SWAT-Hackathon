Campaign Name: Azure AI Platform — Enterprise Developer Adoption Q4 Push

Business Objective: Drive 8,000 new Azure AI Services signups (paid tier conversions from free trial) from enterprise development teams in Q4 FY26, representing a 35% increase over Q3. Secondary objective: generate 2,500 qualified leads for Azure AI Enterprise Agreements from organizations with 50+ developers currently using AWS Bedrock or Google Vertex AI.

Target Audience: Engineering Directors, VP of Platform Engineering, CTOs, and Senior ML Engineers at companies with 200–10,000 employees in technology, financial services, healthcare, and e-commerce verticals. Primary signal: organizations with active Azure subscriptions (compute or storage) but no Azure AI Services consumption — indicating platform familiarity without AI adoption. Secondary signal: GitHub Copilot enterprise license holders not yet using Azure OpenAI Service.

Key Message: Your developers already build on Azure. Now give them the AI platform that meets them where they are — with Azure OpenAI Service, responsible AI guardrails built in, and enterprise-grade security that your compliance team actually approves. No new infrastructure. No separate billing. Just AI that deploys where your code already lives.

Channels: Developer email sequence (segmented: trial users vs. active Azure subscribers vs. competitive wins), LinkedIn (sponsored content targeting engineering leaders + InMail for VP+), GitHub Sponsorships and README placements, Microsoft Learn campaign (certification path promotion), paid search (Google Ads targeting "enterprise AI platform" + competitor terms), Azure.com landing page with interactive demo, developer conference sponsorships (KubeCon, AI Engineer Summit), webinar series ("From PoC to Production: Azure AI at Scale")

Budget: $3.1M total. Email production and automation: $220K. LinkedIn: $680K. GitHub sponsorships: $340K. Microsoft Learn promotion: $180K. Paid search: $520K. Landing page and interactive demo development: $280K. Conference sponsorships: $450K. Webinar production and promotion: $160K. Creative production (video demos, display, copy): $180K. Contingency: $100K.

Timeline: Campaign live by October 6, 2026. Creative brief to agencies by September 8. First draft assets due September 22. Internal review September 29–October 1. Conference materials shipped by September 26 (KubeCon Oct 13–16). Email sequences begin October 6. LinkedIn and paid search go live October 6. Webinar #1: October 15. Webinar #2: November 5. Webinar #3: November 19. Mid-campaign optimization: November 3. Campaign ends December 13. Post-campaign analysis due January 6, 2027.

Success Metrics: New Azure AI Services paid tier activations (target: 8,000). Enterprise Agreement qualified leads (target: 2,500). Email sequence: open rate >28%, CTR >3.5%, trial-to-paid conversion >12%. LinkedIn: CTR >1.4%, cost per qualified lead <$95. Paid search: cost per signup <$65 for brand terms, <$140 for competitor terms. Webinar series: 1,500 registrations total, 45% attendance rate, 20% post-webinar trial activation. GitHub sponsorship: 50,000 impressions, 2% CTR to landing page. Landing page: 8.5% conversion rate (visitor to signup/demo request). Conference: 800 booth conversations, 200 qualified follow-ups. Net Promoter Score of campaign touchpoints: >45.

Constraints / Brand Notes: All messaging must align with Microsoft's "Responsible AI" principles — do not make claims about AI capabilities without referencing safety guardrails. Do not disparage competing platforms by name in public channels (competitive positioning is allowed in 1:1 sales outreach only). Code samples must be production-quality, not toy examples — developers will judge quality immediately. All demo environments must use synthetic data, never real customer data. Video content must include closed captions. Webinar recordings must be available within 48 hours. Landing page must offer both "Start Free" and "Contact Sales" paths — do not force enterprise buyers into self-service flow. GitHub README placements must feel native to developer workflows, not like traditional advertising. Conference booth staff must include actual Azure AI engineers, not just marketing representatives.
